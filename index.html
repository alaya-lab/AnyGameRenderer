Agents and vibe-coding tools can already build a playable game — the layout, logic, motion and camera of every scene. AnyGameRenderer turns that crude recording into the finished game, and one checkpoint covers all three axes of diversity: many game genres — shooters, racers, sports, fantasy and more; many input forms — blockouts, colored primitives, wireframes, semantic maps, coarse mockups; and many output styles — from Ghibli and LEGO to cyberpunk and watercolor — with every entity’s position, timing and count locked to the input.

04

Training data

Paired (crude → styled) clips from three sources, frame-aligned by construction.

3,221 clipsAgent-made games

38 games built end-to-end by an AI coding agent, each exported with five aligned render passes, from blockout to final.

3,910 clipsUnreal Engine

Same simulation rendered twice — blockout and full — with scenarios scripted against zero-shot failure cases.

3,199 clipsReal recordings

Real gameplay with detected 3D boxes re-rendered as crude proxy shapes; the original recording is the target.

05

Does it stay faithful?

Frame-level metrics cannot tell whether each entity is rendered correctly, so we score entity correspondence directly: 59 held-out cases, 753 entities annotated with ground-truth boxes, same seed / steps / captions as the zero-shot base. The GT floor runs the real target footage through the same measurement pipeline.

A single checkpoint improves temporal match in every genre of the benchmark — first-person shooter, top-down, third-person, crowd and vehicle games — while styles stay fully promptable. Full protocol, per-source splits and qualitative comparisons are in the technical report.
